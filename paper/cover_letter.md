Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Who Pays for Pharmaceutical Innovation? A Reproducible Multidimensional Free-Rider Index for Ten High-Income Countries," for publication as an Original Research article in Value in Health.

**Description and interest to readers.** Claims that other countries "free-ride" on US-funded pharmaceutical innovation now drive US most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing policy, the December 2025 UK-US pharmaceutical arrangement, and a Section 301 investigation into Germany. Yet the measures behind these claims are one-dimensional, usually a price ratio. Building on Basu's dynamic-efficiency model (American Journal of Health Economics, 2026) and the public-goods framing of Frech et al (Health Economics, 2026), we construct a five-component index spanning payment (brand-name price; innovative-drug revenue relative to GDP), adoption (share of US-first drugs reimbursed; interval to reimbursement), and domestic pharmaceutical R&D for nine high-income countries, with the United States as reference, and report rank uncertainty across 10,000 specifications with calibrated input noise and alternative data sources, following the OECD/JRC composite-indicator methodology. South Korea and Australia consistently combine low payment with slow, partial adoption; Germany and Switzerland contribute through fast listing and research intensity; Canada, Japan, France, the United Kingdom, and Italy occupy a contested middle. Most importantly, the payment sub-index reproduces the price-based "Freeriding Index" that has informed policy debate (Spearman ρ = 0.89), whereas the adoption sub-index is unrelated to it (ρ = -0.04): price-only measures miss the channel on which countries differ most. The Discussion draws on the market-size, launch-delay, and MFN literatures to argue that dynamic efficiency is a shared responsibility and that payers outside the United States have a direct stake in raising their own contribution. Value in Health has published several recent analyses of international reference pricing, MFN, and cost-effectiveness thresholds; this paper adds a reproducible, adjustable benchmark with an open-source dashboard.

**Contribution to the literature.** To our knowledge this is the first multidimensional, uncertainty-quantified operationalization of international free-riding on pharmaceutical innovation, and the first to decompose its relationship with the single-metric measures published by HHS-ASPE, Frech et al, Kolchinsky and Xie, and Philipson et al in 2025-2026.
