Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Who Pays for Pharmaceutical Innovation? A Reproducible Multidimensional Free-Rider Index for Ten High-Income Countries," for publication as an Original Research article in Value in Health.

**Description and interest to readers.** Claims that other countries "free-ride" on US-funded pharmaceutical innovation now drive US most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing policy, the December 2025 UK-US pharmaceutical arrangement, and a Section 301 investigation into Germany. Yet the measures behind these claims are one-dimensional, usually a price ratio. Building on Basu's dynamic-efficiency model (American Journal of Health Economics, 2026), we construct a five-component index spanning payment (relative price; innovative-drug revenue relative to GDP), access (share of US-first drugs reimbursed; reimbursement delay), and domestic pharmaceutical R&D for ten high-income countries, and we report rank uncertainty across 10,000 specifications following the OECD/JRC composite-indicator methodology. Three findings are robust: South Korea, Australia, and Canada consistently pay less, wait longer, and invest less than peers; Germany and Switzerland contribute through fast access and R&D despite low prices; and Italy, France, the United Kingdom, and Japan cannot be reliably ordered. The ranking is essentially uncorrelated with the price-versus-income "Freeriding Index" that has informed policy debate. Value in Health has published several recent analyses of international reference pricing, MFN, and cost-effectiveness thresholds; this paper adds a reproducible, adjustable benchmark that HTA agencies, payers, and negotiators can apply directly, together with an open-source dashboard.

**Contribution to the literature.** To our knowledge this is the first multidimensional, uncertainty-quantified operationalization of international free-riding on pharmaceutical innovation, and the first to compare such an index systematically with the single-metric measures published by HHS-ASPE, Frech et al, Kolchinsky and Xie, and Philipson et al in 2025-2026.
